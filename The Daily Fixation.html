Papers worth a long fixation

Three picks on how questions, goals and instructions given before reading steer where the eyes go.

  1. Çankaya University · 2024

    The Effect of Integrative Prequestions on Learning From Text: An Eye-Tracking Study

    Thesis study

    Answering integrative questions before reading drew more fixations and more transitions to the prequestioned sentences. Surprisingly, readers also learned the non-prequestioned material better while looking at it less.

    Less attention, more learning. Discuss in your limitations section.
  2. ICLR 2026

    Decoding Open-Ended Information Seeking Goals from Eye Movements in Reading

    Hadar, Berzak and colleagues · Technion

    Readers got a specific question before reading. Using the OneStop dataset (360 readers), multimodal LLMs could tell which question a person had in mind from their eye movements alone, and could partly reconstruct the question's wording.

    The pre-reading prompt leaves fingerprints all over the scanpath.
  3. J. Eye Movement Research · 2025

    Processing Written Language in Video Games: An Eye-Tracking Study on Subtitled Instructions

    JEMR 18(5), 44

    Players saw instructions as subtitles, speech, or both, some meant for them and some for AI teammates. It tracks how attention is split between relevant and irrelevant instructions under time pressure, extending E-Z Reader ideas to multimedia.

    Instruction relevance, but make it a game.
